We suggest that people preferentially imitate the behavior of others they deem to have similar reward functions to their own. We further argue that these inferences can be made on the basis of very sparse or indirect data, by leveraging an inductive bias toward positing the existence of different _groups_ or _types_ of people with similar reward functions, allowing learners to select imitation targets without direct evidence of alignment.
